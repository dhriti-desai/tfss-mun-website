JCC Cuban Missile Crisis: Soviet Union

In October 1962, the Cold War reached its most dangerous moment. When U.S. reconnaissance revealed Soviet nuclear missile sites being built in Cuba, the balance of the Cold War shifted overnight. Nuclear weapons were now just 90 miles from American shores. The discovery came after U.S. reconnaissance flights captured photographic evidence of Soviet missile installations being constructed on the island. The Soviet Union could soon possess the ability to strike much of the United States from just 90 miles away.

With the information still highly classified and the American public unaware of the threat, Kennedy assembled his most trusted military, diplomatic, and intelligence advisors—the Executive Committee of the National Security Council (ExComm). The United States has yet to make its first move.

Thirteen days. Two superpowers. One world on the brink.

As members of the Executive Committee, you are no longer observers of history—you are inside the room where history is being made. Every decision carries consequences: negotiate with the Soviets, impose further pressure, launch military action, pursue covert operations, or risk nuclear escalation.

There is no script, no guaranteed outcome, and no second chance. The missiles are real. The clock is ticking. And the next move is yours.
